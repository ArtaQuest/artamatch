The model, precisely

One formula scores everything on this page — the date ranking and the two-chart reading alike:

score = bias + Σ over 32 angles of  Σ over the five aspects A of  w[angle,A] · cos(θ − A)

        A ∈ { conjunction 0° · sextile 60° · square 90° · trine 120° · opposition 180° }

p     = sigmoid(score)

θ is always the angle between two sidereal longitudes (Lahiri, cast at 12:00 UT on the birth date): his planet to hers, or two planets inside one chart. The five aspect angles are fixed — the five Ptolemaic majors, written down before any data was read: not fitted, not chosen by a search, the same five on every pair. What is fitted is how much weight each aspect carries on each pair. Those five weights are not five free numbers, and this is the part worth checking by hand: they are a function of the pair's two, by w[angle,A] = (2/5)·amp·cos(A − ideal separation), which sums back to ΣA w[angle,A]·cos(θ−A) = amp·cos(θ − ideal separation) exactly. Two consequences you can verify in the published weights: conjunction and opposition always come out exact negatives of each other, because cos(θ−180°) = −cosθ; and because ΣA e2iA = 0 for these five angles — they are a balanced set — the penalty the basis puts on a pair is isotropic, so choosing to write the model in these five aspects privileges no direction of the sky. There is nothing else in it — no buckets, no indicators, no inputs beyond the two birth dates. It answers one narrow question: did the historical record list children for a couple like this, over … finished marriages, every birth date a real recorded day.

The angles come from six families, and four of the six need only one of the two charts: his planet to hers (his[i] − her[j]) and their sum (his[i] + her[j]) read the couple, while his own natal aspects (his[i] − his[j]), hers (her[i] − her[j]) and each partner’s own midpoint sums (his[i] + his[j], her[i] + her[j]) read one person alone. An earlier edition searched only the couple angles; that restriction was lifted on 4 September 2026, and every family earned its way in. Which ones the model actually kept is in ….

The model was chosen from 4,225 candidate angles by forward selection — each candidate scored by the exact score test, against what the model already holds, so an angle that merely restates an earlier one cannot get in — and its number is earned the hard way: the selection, the size of the model and the inclusion of every fast body were re-run from scratch inside each of ten training folds, so the quoted AUC never saw its own test couples.

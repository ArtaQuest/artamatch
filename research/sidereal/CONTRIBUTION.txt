NOT IN THE GIANT MODEL, and absent rather than zero:
  physics    needs birthplaces; location was removed from this edition, so it has nothing to read
  traditions the 21-module chart sweep (vedic, hellenistic, uranian, harmonics, ...) is ~50,000 raw
             features across its modules — too wide to concatenate into one matrix at this row count.
             It was built and measured separately; under the same control it sits at chance.

The yardstick is WHICH DAY OF THE MONTH the wedding was written down on: 0.5101 controlled.
It carries no astrological content whatsoever. Read every row above against it.

Columns: 'alone' is plain ages plus that family and nothing else. 'add gain' is what it adds to the ages.
'LOFO cost' is what the giant model loses when that family is removed — the honest marginal number, and
far smaller than 'add gain' wherever the families are redundant with each other, which is nearly always.

THE SPREAD BETWEEN THE TWO RUNS IS 0.0076. THE LARGEST MARGIN ANY FAMILY ACHIEVES OVER THE YARDSTICK IS
+0.0015. The disagreement between two runs of the same code on the same data is FIVE TIMES the best effect
either run claims.

So the honest reading is not "zodiac clears the bar by +0.0015 on the GPU run". It is that nothing here is
resolved: the top-three ordering flips between runs (zodiac/geometry/world2 against geometry/zodiac/maya), and
four of the nine LOFO costs change SIGN — geometry's marginal value is +0.0123 on one run and +0.0041 on the
other, world2's is +0.0055 and -0.0012.

Either table alone would have supported a confident ranking of the world's astrological traditions. Run twice,
they agree on only one thing: none of these systems is distinguishable from the day of the month a clerk
happened to write down, and they are not distinguishable from each other either.

How to read the three columns

Each row is one feature and one two-parameter logistic.

Train is the feature's AUC on 25,000 training couples born 1600–1900, and it is the only column used to order anything. Held out is the same feature on 13,250 couples born after 1900, with b₁'s sign taken from the training half — never re-chosen using the answers. Gap-matched is the held-out AUC computed within one-year age-gap bands.

That third column is the one that matters. The age gap alone scores 0.6047, so a feature that merely encodes the difference between two dates scores well while saying nothing astrological. A slow planet's separation between two charts is exactly such an encoding: Saturn moves 12.2° a year, so Saturn-to-Saturn separation is the age gap, wrapped — it tops the raw table at 0.5700 and falls to 0.4942 once the gap is held flat.

Because 667 features were searched, the largest of that many pure-null draws is expected near 0.5222. Read the top of the table against that, not against 0.50. Six features clear it — and all six are Saturn-or-slower longitudes, while the birth year itself scores 0.5258 under the same control. They are the era wearing a planet's name.
